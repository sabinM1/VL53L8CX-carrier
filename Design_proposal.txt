**Assumptions (explicit)**
1. Five identical carrier boards, each with its own sensor, 1.8 V LDO, U3, U5 and a 1×6 header.
2. SCLK, MOSI and MISO are a shared 3.3 V bus; five NCS lines are separate.
3. Sensor worst-case current is 150 mA peak and 93 mA typical per board on the 3.3 V rail (Table 14 [V Rev 1]). Peaks from different sensors are assumed to coincide (conservative).
4. Wiring is short bench or small-harness wiring, total SCLK length ≲ 20 cm (§5.2).
5. DS14161 Rev 11 is the sensor reference.
6. "RP2350-Zero Mini" and Waveshare's "RP2350-Zero" are treated as the same electrical board (same 29-GPIO, ME6217 description on both pages [V]). A Mini-specific board definition was not verified.

## 0. Executive summary

1. **Architecture.** Five identical carriers. Each has a VL53L8CX in SPI mode, a local MIC5504-1.8 V LDO (CORE_1V8 + IOVDD), a SN74AXC4T774 (U3) for SCLK/MOSI/MISO, and a SN74AXC1T45 (U5) for NCS. All five share SCLK, MOSI and MISO to an RP2350-Zero Mini on SPI1; each has its own NCS GPIO.
2. **OE→NCS_S works only if NCS bypasses the gated shifter.** U3's OE is tied to NCS_S (active-low OE: NCS high disables outputs, so MISO is Hi-Z when unselected). NCS_S is produced by U5, not U3. Tying OE to NCS_S while NCS also passes through U3 deadlocks (§2.1).
3. **The onboard 800 mA LDO cannot power the array.** Peak sensor load 750 mA plus host ≈ 40 mA leaves ~10 mA margin (1.3 %); with the WS2812 lit the load exceeds the rating. A linear regulator from 5 V would dissipate ~1.3 W peak and draws that current from USB (limit 500 mA). **Use an external ≥1.5 A 3.3 V supply for the sensor boards; power the RP2350-Zero from USB-C; common ground; never join the two 3.3 V rails.**
4. **Pins.** SPI1: GP10 (SCK), GP11 (MOSI), GP12 (MISO). NCS1–5: GP2–GP6. All in the GP0–GP15 range. I assumed those are among the 20 edge pins, **[U] until checked against Waveshare's pinout image** (the text of the wiki gives only a picture).
5. **Firmware.** STM32duino VL53L8CX is documented as "compatible with all architectures", but I found **no RP2040/RP2350 SPI report** and its README does not mention them. Arduino-Pico lists "Waveshare RP2350 Zero" as a supported board. Treat first bring-up with one board as the real test (§6).
6. **Carried over from Rev 1 unchanged:** the translator cannot be removed (IOVDD abs max 1.98 V), the 1.8 V rail must rise 0.9→1.6 V in **≤58 µs** (DS14161 Rev 12 Table 5: 0.012 V/µs min; the MIC5504 does not guarantee this at max — see §4), SPI mode 3 at ≤3 MHz, five 47 kΩ pull-ups to IOVDD, no wet cleaning.

## 2. B. Architecture and the OE→NCS_S correction

### 2.1 Why NCS must not pass through the gated shifter

Requested scheme: U3.OE = NCS_S, where NCS_S is the sensor-side NCS net.

1. NCS_S idles high (R1 47 kΩ to 1V8). OE high puts all of U3's outputs in Hi-Z, including the NCS channel (A3).
2. The host drives NCS_H low. The NCS channel is disabled, so NCS_S does not follow. NCS_S stays high, OE stays high.
3. The sensor is never selected. The state is stable and unrecoverable by firmware.

**Fix (implemented below):** produce NCS_S with U5, a separate SN74AXC1T45 with no enable pin. NCS_H → U5 → NCS_S → {sensor C2, R1, U3.OE}. U3 carries only SCLK, MOSI and MISO. This keeps the requested behaviour (NCS high = U3 Hi-Z, MISO released) and works.

### 2.2 Behaviour table (per board)

| NCS_H (3.3 V) | NCS_S (1.8 V) | U3 (OE) | SCLK_S / MOSI_S | MISO_H |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High (not selected) | High | Hi-Z | SCLK_S held high by R6; MOSI_S Hi-Z | Hi-Z (does not load the shared net) |
| Low (selected) | Low | Enabled | Driven by host | Driven by sensor MISO |

### 2.3 Why R6 (SCLK_S pull-up) exists

When U3 is disabled, SCLK_S is Hi-Z. On selection, SCLK_S would be driven high by the host (mode 3 idles high) a few ns after NCS_S falls, creating a **rising SCLK edge while NCS is low**, which the sensor could sample as a clock. R6 to 1V8 holds SCLK_S at its idle level so no edge occurs. **R6 = 6.8 kΩ** (Rev 4; was 10 kΩ): SCES898C §7.3.9 gives 71 kΩ typical static pull-downs on the AXC4T774 data I/Os [V], so the idle level is 1.8 × 71/77.8 = **1.64 V** instead of 1.58 V with 10 kΩ. Both clear the sensor's V_IH (0.7 × 1.8 = 1.26 V, DS14161 Table 15), but 6.8 kΩ adds margin for the same part count. TI also states that any external **pull-up** on an AXC data I/O must be ≤ 7 kΩ, which 6.8 kΩ satisfies. Cost: ~0.26 mA only while SCLK is low and driven. This is an engineering addition, not a datasheet requirement; scope it.

### 2.4 Why R7 (NCS_H pull-up) is now mandatory

RP2350 GPIO pads reset with the internal pull-down enabled [U, RP2350 datasheet from memory, check]. Without R7, all five NCS_H lines are low at reset, so all five sensors are selected and five translators are enabled onto one MISO net until firmware runs. R7 = 4.7 kΩ to 3V3 holds NCS_H high. Firmware must still drive NCS high as its first action.

**⚠ Rev 3 (round-2 review): R7 back-feeds the dead rail.** R7 references the board's 3V3_EXT. With the host on and the external rail off, each GPIO drives ~0.7 mA through R7 into the 3V3 node (~3.5 mA for five), partially powering AVDD and the LDO input; CORE_1V8 can then creep up slowly and never see the 0.012 V/µs edge. With the rail on and the host off, R7 pushes current into the RP2350 pad clamps with IOVDD at 0 V. Ioff on the shifter does not cover a resistor path. Fix: move the five pull-ups to the host side (referenced to the host's own 3V3) or add a host-controlled load switch on 3V3_EXT.

### 2.5 System topology

```
RP2350-Zero Mini (USB-C powered, 3.3 V logic from its own LDO)
  GP10 SCK  ───────┬────────┬────────┬────────┬────────┐  (shared)
  GP11 MOSI ───────┤        │        │        │        │
  GP12 MISO ◄──────┤        │        │        │        │
  GP2  NCS1 ──► Board 1     │        │        │        │
  GP3  NCS2 ─────────────► Board 2    │        │        │
  GP4  NCS3 ──────────────────────► Board 3   │        │
  GP5  NCS4 ───────────────────────────────► Board 4    │
  GP6  NCS5 ────────────────────────────────────────► Board 5
  GND ─────────────── common GND ──────────────────────────────
External 3.3 V rail (≥1.5 A) ─► J1-1 of each board (star or bus, short, heavy copper)
```

Each board's J1 is 1×6: 1 = 3V3_EXT, 2 = GND, 3 = SCLK, 4 = MOSI, 5 = MISO, 6 = NCS (this board's line). INT is not routed in this design; poll with `check_data_ready()` (five INT lines would cost five more GPIOs).

### 3.2 Pin count check

Needed: 3 shared SPI + 5 NCS = **8 GPIO**. Available on the edge (assumed): 20. Pass, with 12 spare. GP16 (WS2812) is not used.

### 3.3 Power budget: RP2350 + 5 sensors vs. the 800 mA LDO

**Sensor load (3.3 V rail), from Rev 1 Table 14 [V Rev 1]**
- Per board: peak 150 mA; typical 43 + 50 = 93 mA (AVDD 43/50, CORE_1V8 50/80, +10 mA each peak).
- Five boards: peak 5 × 150 = **750 mA**; typical 5 × 93 = **465 mA**.
- Pull-ups, shifters, R7: < 3 mA total (R7 sinks up to 0.7 mA per board only while NCS is low). Neglected.

**Host load (assumption, [U], measure):** RP2350 at 150 MHz, flash, USB and idle LED ≈ 40 mA. Upper bound with the WS2812 at full white ≈ 100 mA (the LED alone ~60 mA [U]).

**Budget against the onboard LDO (800 mA)**

| Case | Load | Margin to 800 mA |
|---|---|---|
| Peak, LED off | 750 + 40 = **790 mA** | **10 mA (1.3 %)** |
| Peak, LED on | 750 + 100 = 850 mA | **−50 mA (over rating)** |
| Typical, LED off | 465 + 40 = **505 mA** | 295 mA (63 % of rating) |

**Dissipation at 5 V in** (linear LDO: input current ≈ output current)
- Typical: (5.0 − 3.3) × 0.505 = **0.86 W**
- Peak: (5.0 − 3.3) × 0.79 = **1.34 W**
- ME6217C33M5G is a SOT-23-5 part (the "M5" suffix, [U]). I did not retrieve its power-dissipation or θJA figure; SOT-23-5 LDOs are usually rated for a few tenths of a watt [U]. Even at the typical case the dissipation is likely 2× or more above what the package can shed. **Check the Microne datasheet before relying on this, but the conclusion does not depend on it** (next point).

**USB budget:** the LDO input comes from VBUS (USB-C). Peak input ≈ 790 mA; typical ≈ 505 mA. A USB 2.0 port allows 500 mA after enumeration (100 mA before) [standard, U for this board's negotiation behaviour].

**Verdict: the margin is too narrow, and the design fails on three independent counts** (current margin 1.3 %, thermal dissipation, USB current). The summed peak is conservative (five sensors need not peak simultaneously), but the typical case alone already consumes 63 % of the LDO rating and all of the USB allowance, and the design should not depend on staggering.

### 3.4 Recommended power architecture (mandatory)

1. **RP2350-Zero Mini powered from USB-C only.** Its onboard LDO supplies only the RP2350 and its own logic (≈ 40–100 mA).
2. **Sensor boards powered from a separate external 3.3 V rail,** feeding J1-1 on all five boards.
   - Rating: ≥ 1.5 A (2× the 750 mA peak). Regulation 3.3 V ± 3 % (AVDD window 3.13–3.47 V [V Rev 1]) at the boards, so allow for harness drop: 150 mA × ~0.1 Ω ≈ 15 mV per board path.
   - Use a **buck (switching) 3.3 V converter from a separate ≥ 5 V, ≥ 2 A source, not a linear regulator.** A 5→3.3 V linear regulator would burn (5 − 3.3) × 0.465 ≈ 0.8 W typical.
   - **Do not source this converter from the RP2350's USB VBUS** if the host PC is the supply: peak 3.3 V × 0.75 A / (5 V × ~0.9) ≈ 0.55 A exceeds 500 mA. Use a wall supply.
   - Add ≥ 47 µF bulk near the distribution point; each board already has C1 4.7 µF and C6 2.2 µF.
3. **Common ground:** connect external-rail GND to the RP2350 GND at one point.
4. **Never connect the external 3.3 V rail to the RP2350's 3V3 pin.** Two regulators on one node can back-feed the ME6217 or fight.
5. **Sequencing:** **not** any order — R7 breaks this (see §2.4). Below 100 mV on either VCC the AXC outputs go Hi-Z and Ioff gives partial power-down protection [V TI features], but R7 is a resistor path that Ioff does not cover. With the host on and the rail off, R7 back-feeds the rail; with the rail on and the host off, R7 back-feeds the RP2350 pads. Fix R7 (host-side pull-up or a host-controlled load switch on 3V3_EXT) before relying on this item.
6. **VCCB is the external 3.3 V rail** and the RP2350 outputs are nominally 3.3 V from a different regulator. AXC inputs tolerate up to 3.6 V [V Rev 1]; the two 3.3 V rails differ by a few percent at most.

### 3.5 Performance

150 MHz is more than enough for 3 MHz SPI. The limit is the 3 MHz sensor SPI clock, not the host. Firmware download is ~84 KB per sensor, ≈ 0.23 s at 3 MHz [calc], ≈ 1.2 s for five sensors in sequence, plus sensor boot time [U].

### 3.6 SPI pin mapping on the RP2350-Zero Mini

SPI1 is used (SPI0's default pins in Zephyr are GP20–GP23, which are pad-only [S]).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| SCLK | **GP10** | SPI1 SCK | 3 | shared |
| MOSI | **GP11** | SPI1 TX | 4 | shared |
| MISO | **GP12** | SPI1 RX | 5 | shared, no internal pull on the pad |
| NCS1 | **GP2** | GPIO output | board 1, pin 6 | idle high |

## 4. D. Regulator comparison (1.8 V only; host LDO noted)

Variant B parts (TPS7A2033, TLV755P-3.3) are deleted. The 1.8 V rail per board carries ~90 mA peak (CORE_1V8 80 + 10 mA [V Rev 1]).

| Part | Package | Iout | Start-up | θJA | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MIC5504-1.8YMT** (Microchip) | 4-pin DFN 1.0×1.0 | 300 mA | **50 µs typ / 125 µs max** [V Rev 1, DS20006006C] | 250 °C/W [V Rev 1] | **Chosen, but does NOT guarantee the 58 µs rule at max (125 µs); typ 50 µs is only ~14 % under.** See §4 caveat. |
| LDK130M18R (ST) | DFN6 1.2×1.3 | 300 mA | not documented in what I retrieved | n/a | Fallback; used on ST's SATEL-VL53L8 [V Rev 1]. |
| TPS7A2018 (TI) | X2SON-4 | 300 mA | ~750 µs typ [S/U] | 166.1 °C/W (DQN) | Only if a scope shows ≤58 µs (it does not: 750 µs typ). |
| *Host:* ME6217C33M5G | SOT-23-5 [U] | **800 mA** | n/a | not retrieved | Powers the RP2350 only (§3.4). |

- Input is the board's 3V3 (external rail), **EN tied to IN — this is a problem, see below.**
- Dissipation per board: (3.3 − 1.8) × 0.09 = **135 mW**; rise ≈ 34 °C at 250 °C/W (Rev 1). At +85 °C ambient that is ~119 °C against the 125 °C limit: acceptable, scope it. Five boards each carry their own, so no shared-thermal issue.
- **⚠ Rev 3 (round-2 review):** with EN = IN the LDO output follows the 3V3_EXT ramp until it regulates. A buck with ms-scale soft-start (3.3 V over 1 ms = 0.0033 V/µs) violates the 0.012 V/µs CORE_1V8 minimum on its own, and the MIC5504's own 125 µs max exceeds the 58 µs limit. Fix: drive EN from a delayed/power-good signal so the LDO starts after 3V3 is stable, and choose an external supply with ≤ ~3 ms soft-start (AVDD limit 0.001 V/µs). Scope CORE_1V8 on every board.

## 5. E. Level-shifter comparison and the OE modification

### 5.1 U3: SN74AXC4T774 (RSV UQFN-16), SCLK / MOSI / MISO only

| Aspect | Rev 1 | Rev 2 |
|---|---|---|
| OE (pin 7) | GND | **NCS_S** (1.8 V, from U5) |
| Channels used | 4 (SCLK, MOSI, NCS, MISO) | **3** (SCLK, MOSI, MISO) |
| Channel 3 (A3 pin 3 / B3 pin 10) | NCS | **Idle:** B3 → GND, A3 not connected, DIR3 → GND. The channel is driven deterministically low and nothing reads it. |
| MISO behaviour when unselected | Driven low by internal pull-down (the contention case) | **Hi-Z** (OE high) |

Alternatives for the OE→NCS_S requirement:
- A separate 74xx125-type buffer only on MISO [S, forum suggestion]: more parts per board than adding U5.
- Merging MISO through a mux: overkill [S].

### 5.2 U5: SN74AXC1T45 (new), NCS only

| Pin | Name | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | VCCA | 1V8 (+ C8 100 nF) |
| 2 | GND | GND |
| 3 | A | **NCS_S** (to U1.C2, R1, U3 pin 7) |
| 4 | B | **NCS_H** (J1-6, R7) |
| 5 | DIR | GND (B→A: host to sensor) |
| 6 | VCCB | 3V3 (+ C9 100 nF) |

Pin numbers [V TI SCES882E Table 4-1; re-confirmed from the `sn74axc1t45` conversion in `reference docs/components/`]. DIR low = B→A [V]. Package: DCK (SC70-6) preferred for size, DBV (SOT-23-6) easier to hand-solder, DTQ (X2SON-6 1.0 × 0.8 mm) smallest; orderable suffix to confirm at the distributor [U]. Propagation delay and DIR reference rail: DIR is referenced to VCCA [V]; propagation delay not retrieved [U].

### 5.3 Bus-level notes
- Enable time of U3 after NCS_S falls is nanoseconds-to-tens-of-ns [U, not retrieved]; the host's first SCLK edge follows NCS by ≥ ~1 µs when NCS is a software GPIO, so margin is large. Do not drive NCS with a hardware CS that asserts within a few ns of the first clock.
- Five AXC inputs on SCLK and MOSI: ~few pF each [U]; at 3 MHz with ≲ 20 cm total wire this is fine. Keep SCLK away from MISO. Optional 22 Ω series resistors at the RP2350 end of SCLK and MOSI (host side, one set only) if ringing appears (TI warns the AXC drive can ring on light loads [V Rev 1]).
- The five idle MISO outputs are Hi-Z (OE high). If their internal pull-downs stay on in Hi-Z [U], the host sees ≈ 71 kΩ / 5 ≈ 14 kΩ to GND on MISO while no board is selected; the selected shifter drives that without difficulty (≈ 0.23 mA at 3.3 V).

| Waveshare's own instructions | Wiki: add the Arduino-Pico index URL (v4.5.2 shown), install "Raspberry Pi Pico/RP2040/RP2350", then choose a Pico model such as Raspberry Pi Pico 2. It does **not** name a separate Zero entry. This conflicts mildly with the README list. | [V] |

**Conclusion.** The library's "all architectures" label is **not evidence** that SPI works on RP2350. The Portenta failure points to a likely trap on ArduinoCore-API cores (`SPIClass` is an abstract `HardwareSPI`, so the library cannot construct one). Arduino-Pico is also ArduinoCore-API-based [U], so the code below passes a **pointer to the ready-made `SPI1` object** and never constructs an SPIClass. **Status: unproven until one board ranges on your RP2350-Zero.** Fallbacks, in order: (1) patch the library's platform layer; (2) port ST's ULD `Platform` SPI functions yourself (as Rev 1 §3 already assumed for Teensy; UM3109 §3.1 [V Rev 1]).

### 6.3 Five-sensor polling sketch (continuous mode)

**Read this before compiling.** I could not retrieve `vl53l8cx.h` (GitHub raw/blob URLs were blocked in this session). The constructor signature and method names below follow the pattern of the STM32duino examples **from memory and are [U]**. The task text uses `check_data_ready()`, consistent with it. Confirm every call against `src/vl53l8cx.h`. In particular verify (a) the SPI constructor argument order, (b) whether `lpn_pin = -1` is accepted (LPn is pulled to IOVDD on the board, with no host line), (c) the SPI clock default (must be ≤ 3 MHz, mode 3), (d) the return types of `begin()`/`init()`.

```cpp
// 5x VL53L8CX on a shared SPI1 bus, one NCS GPIO per board.  RP2350-Zero Mini, Arduino-Pico core.
// Hardware: each board's SN74AXC4T774 (U3) has OE tied to that board's NCS_S.  NCS_S comes from
// U5 (SN74AXC1T45), so NCS low = board selected = U3 enabled; NCS high = U3 Hi-Z (MISO released).
#include <Arduino.h>
#include <SPI.h>
#include <vl53l8cx.h>                       // STM32duino VL53L8CX  [API names: verify against header]

constexpr uint8_t  NUM_SENSORS   = 5;
constexpr uint8_t  PIN_SCK       = 10;      // SPI1 SCK
constexpr uint8_t  PIN_MOSI      = 11;      // SPI1 TX
constexpr uint8_t  PIN_MISO      = 12;      // SPI1 RX
constexpr uint8_t  NCS_PINS[NUM_SENSORS] = {2, 3, 4, 5, 6};
constexpr uint32_t SPI_HZ        = 3000000; // sensor limit: 3 MHz, mode 3 (DS14161 Table 8)
constexpr uint8_t  RANGING_HZ    = 15;      // per sensor, 8x8
constexpr uint8_t  CENTER_ZONE   = 27;      // one of the four central zones of the 8x8 grid

VL53L8CX*            sensors[NUM_SENSORS];
VL53L8CX_ResultsData results;               // reused buffer; 64 zones

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  while (!Serial && millis() < 3000) {}

  // 1) FIRST: park every NCS high so no board is selected (all U3 Hi-Z, MISO bus idle).
  //    R7 on each board already holds NCS high at reset; this keeps it high in firmware.
  for (uint8_t i = 0; i < NUM_SENSORS; i++) {
    pinMode(NCS_PINS[i], OUTPUT);
    digitalWrite(NCS_PINS[i], HIGH);
  }

  // 2) Shared bus. Pass a pointer to the existing SPI1 object. Do NOT construct an SPIClass
  //    (that is the call that fails on ArduinoCore-API cores, see the Portenta thread).
  SPI1.setSCK(PIN_SCK);
  SPI1.setTX(PIN_MOSI);
  SPI1.setRX(PIN_MISO);
  SPI1.begin();

  // 3) Create, initialise and start each sensor one at a time.
  //    Each init downloads ~84 KB over SPI: about 0.23 s per sensor at 3 MHz.
  for (uint8_t i = 0; i < NUM_SENSORS; i++) {
    sensors[i] = new VL53L8CX(&SPI1, NCS_PINS[i], -1 /*LPn: pulled up on board*/, -1 /*no I2C reset*/);
    sensors[i]->begin();
    // Inside every transaction the library drops NCS_i. That falls on NCS_S, enables U3 (OE low),
    // and connects this sensor's MISO to the shared bus. When NCS returns high, U3 goes Hi-Z.
    if (sensors[i]->init() != 0) {                    // [verify return convention]
      Serial.printf("sensor %u: init FAILED\n", i);
      continue;                                       // leave this one out; others keep running
    }
    sensors[i]->set_resolution(VL53L8CX_RESOLUTION_8X8);
    sensors[i]->set_ranging_frequency_hz(RANGING_HZ);
    sensors[i]->start_ranging();                      // continuous mode
    Serial.printf("sensor %u: ranging at %u Hz\n", i, RANGING_HZ);
  }
}

void loop() {
  for (uint8_t i = 0; i < NUM_SENSORS; i++) {
    if (sensors[i] == nullptr) continue;

    uint8_t ready = 0;
    // "Selecting" sensor i = NCS_i low for the duration of this call. The hardware link
    // OE(U3) = NCS_S(i) enables ONLY this board's shifter, so exactly one MISO driver is active.
    sensors[i]->check_data_ready(&ready);

    if (ready) {
      sensors[i]->get_ranging_data(&results);         // NCS_i low again for this transfer
      // distance_mm is indexed [zone * VL53L8CX_NB_TARGET_PER_ZONE + target]
      int16_t mm = results.distance_mm[VL53L8CX_NB_TARGET_PER_ZONE * CENTER_ZONE];
      Serial.printf("S%u zone%u: %d mm (status %u)\n", i, CENTER_ZONE, mm,
                    results.target_status[VL53L8CX_NB_TARGET_PER_ZONE * CENTER_ZONE]);
    }
  }
  delay(2);   // poll faster than the 15 Hz frame rate; each poll is a short SPI transaction
}
```

Notes on the sketch
- **NCS handling is inside the library** in my recollection of its platform layer [U]. If it turns out the library does not toggle NCS, wrap each call in `digitalWrite(NCS_PINS[i], LOW)` / `HIGH` yourself and add `delayMicroseconds(1)` after the falling edge so U5 and U3's OE have settled.
- Do not enable an internal pull on MISO or on NCS pads. **RP2350-E9:** a weak-input pad with the input buffer enabled can sit near mid-rail on early silicon. CNX Software reports the A4 stepping fixes E9 (parts marked RP2350A0A4 / RP2350B0A4); the failure mode and the A2 workaround were not retrieved [S]. Check the stepping on your Zero Mini; if it is A2, add a defined bias on MISO_H at the host (pull-up to 3V3, per the E9 text) and verify against the RP2350 datasheet.
- RAM: five driver contexts plus result buffers are a few kB each [U], trivial against 520 KB.
- Add `Serial` timing output the first time and confirm no frame is dropped at 5 × 15 Hz.

## 9. I. Documentation

### 9.1 Timing
- Rev 1 results hold per board: AXC delay ≤ 5 ns (host→sensor) and ≤ 7 ns (MISO) at 1.8/3.3 V [V Rev 1]; MISO path ≈ 27 ns vs a 167 ns half-period at 3 MHz.
- New: U5 adds its own delay to NCS (not retrieved, ns-scale [U]); OE enable time of U3 after NCS_S falls (not retrieved [U]). Both are small against a ≥ 1 µs software NCS-to-clock gap.

### 9.2 Layout and thermal (per board)
- Rev 1 rules unchanged: decoupling within a few mm, shifter next to the sensor, 8 thermal vias, bottom mask open and one paste aperture [V Rev 1, AN5897], 1.8 V LDO in a copper pour.
- Keep **NCS_S short** and away from SCLK_S: it drives U3's OE and the sensor.
- Put U5 beside U3 so NCS_S is a few mm long.
- Bus wiring: daisy-chain SCLK/MOSI/MISO; keep total ≲ 20 cm; ground return alongside each line; a ground wire per board.
- External rail: heavy copper or wire, one star point near the supply, ≥ 47 µF bulk there. Peak 750 mA at 3.3 V ≈ 2.5 W; use ≥ 1.5 A rated connectors.

### 9.3 Power and thermal summary (system)
- Sensor power ≈ 215 mW typ / 320 mW max per sensor [V Rev 1, AN5897]; five sensors: 1.1 W typ / 1.6 W max, **sensor-only**.
- **The external rail carries more than the sensors:** it also feeds the five 1.8 V LDOs. Rail-side: 465 mA typ × 3.3 V ≈ 1.5 W typ, 750 mA peak × 3.3 V ≈ 2.5 W peak. Size the supply and connectors from these numbers, not the sensor-only figure.
- **Teensy 4.0:** ≈ 0.13 W (40 mA × 3.3 V) from its own USB-side regulator. Comfortable. Its 3.3 V pin (~250 mA) is **not** used by the carrier.
- **Load switch drop:** R_ON 53 mΩ typ / 59 mΩ max at 25 °C / 107 mΩ max over −40…+105 °C ⇒ ≤ 44 mV typ, **≤ 80 mV worst case** at 750 mA; 22 µF at VIN plus ≥ 47 µF upstream at the regulator.
- External buck at 3.3 V, 0.75 A peak, ~90 % efficient: ≈ 0.28 W loss.

### 9.4 Optical integration and handling
AN5939 Rev 3 Table 5 [V]: air gap + glass < 1.5 mm; air gap < 0.5 mm without a gasket; gasket needed above 0.7 mm; crosstalk ≤ 300 kcps/SPAD; emitter and receiver 4 mm apart. MSL 3, 25 N maximum, Class 1 laser. **Reflow: DS14161 Rev 12 Table 24 (p. 38) gives 240 °C recommended / 260 °C maximum, 3 passes, convection only — not vapour phase.** **No wet cleaning or coating:** the supporting statement is in the PCB manufacturing guidelines (washing and coating not recommended), **not** TN1532 (the provided TN1532 is a liquid-level note). Five sensors on one assembly: keep **adjacent sensors' fields of view from overlapping** (multi-emitter crosstalk is not characterized here [U]; the sensor emits at 940 nm and sensors share no sync line in this design). **If a cover glass is used, the firmware must call `calibrate_xtalk` / `set_caldata_xtalk`.**

### 9.5 Firmware notes
- Calibration: run per-unit crosstalk calibration with a target ≥ 600 mm [V Rev 1, UM3109 §3.2].
- Hardware reset: no host reset line to the sensors. **The 3V3_EXT load switch is the reset** — it drops AVDD and CORE_1V8 together, as DS14161 requires. `vl53l8cx_init()` begins with a software reboot (verified in `vl53l8cx_api.c`), so a re-init recovers a running sensor; a hung sensor needs the rail cycle.
- Never apply 5 V to IOVDD. Never apply more than 1.98 V to CORE_1V8/IOVDD. All Teensy GPIO is 3.3 V and only meets sensor pins through U3/U5.

**Residual risks**
1. **OE/NCS scheme depends on U5 and R6/R7.** If you revert to a single shifter carrying NCS, the board cannot be selected (§2.1).
2. **Library and SPI were unproven on the RP2350.** Moot: the host is a Teensy 4.0 (core 1.62.0) and all three sketches compile.
3. **1.8 V rail slew** per board (Rev 1 main risk, unchanged; limit is 58.3 µs, not 63 µs). Superseded by the load-switch decision (§13.9).
4. **Edge-pin assignment** of the GPIOs is moot (Teensy header map, §13.3).
5. **U3/U5 pin numbers** — **both confirmed.** U5: VCCA 1, GND 2, A 3, B 4, DIR 5, VCCB 6 (DIR = GND ⇒ B→A). MIC5504 also confirmed (VOUT 1, GND 2, EN 3, VIN 4, EP). **Closed.**
6. **R7 back-feed** makes "any power-up order" false; the 3V3_EXT load switch is the fix (§13.9).
5. **Worst-case current** assumes simultaneous 150 mA peaks; a real array may stay lower, but the supply should be sized for 750 mA.
6. **Items I could not retrieve (Rev 7 status):** *resolved* — AXC4T774 tEN/tDIS (SCES898C §5.11: t_EN ≤ 26 ns, t_DIS ≤ 20 ns) and pull-down behaviour with OE high (71 kΩ static, §7.3.9); AXC1T45 DIR reference rail (VCCA) and pin numbers; MIC5504 pin numbers; `vl53l8cx.h` constructor and API (all verified against the vendored source); TPS22918-Q1 R_ON, ON V_IH, QOD and t_R(CT open) (§13.15). *Still open, bench-only* — the load switch's t_D at CT = 0, its reverse-current behaviour, and t_R under the real 36.5 µF load.

## 13. Host migration to Teensy 4.0 + dual-mode operation

The host is changed from the Waveshare RP2350-Zero Mini to the **Teensy 4.0**
(NXP i.MX RT1062, 600 MHz, 1024 KB RAM, 3.3 V I/O, not 5 V tolerant). The carrier
is unchanged: it still shifts 3.3 V <-> 1.8 V (sensor IOVDD max 1.98 V) and the
sensors still run from the external 3.3 V rail.

### 13.1 Why the Teensy 4.0

- **3 SPI ports** (LPSPI4, LPSPI3, LPSPI1) and **3 I2C ports** (LPI2C1, LPI2C3,
  LPI2C4), all independent controllers [V PJRC spec page].
- The six default pin sets do not overlap, so all can run at once [V core
  headers]: SPI 10/11/12/13, SPI1 0/1/26/27, SPI2 34/35/36/37 (bottom pads),
  Wire 18/19, Wire1 16/17 (alt 36/37), Wire2 24/25.
- **3.3 V pin is limited to ~250 mA external** [V PJRC]. Five sensors peak at
  750 mA, so the **external 3.3 V rail remains mandatory**. The carrier's J1
  carries only `3V3_EXT`, which powers the shifter high side and the LDO; the
  Teensy 3.3 V pin is not used by the carrier.

### 13.2 Mode A — single bus, sequential

All five sensors on **SPI** (SCK 13, MOSI 11, MISO 12), one CS each
(10,9,8,7,6). These are all header pins (0–23). The carrier's OE->NCS_S scheme
keeps exactly one board driving MISO, so sequential polling is safe.

### 13.3 Mode B — 3 SPI + 2 I2C, concurrent

One sensor per bus:

| Sensor | Bus | SCK/SCL | MOSI/SDA | MISO | CS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | SPI (LPSPI4) | 13 | 11 | 12 | 10 |
| S1 | SPI1 (LPSPI3) | 27 | 26 | 1 | 0 |
| S2 | SPI2 (LPSPI1) | 37 | 35 | 34 | 36 |
| S3 | Wire (LPI2C1) | 19 | 18 | – | – |
| S4 | Wire1 (LPI2C3) | 16 | 17 | – | – |

- Each sensor owns a controller, so the five range **concurrently**; the MCU
  drains each bus in turn. A slow I2C read does not stall the SPI sensors.
- **S1 (26/27) and S2 (34–37) are on the bottom SMT pads**; only pins 0–23 are
  breadboardable. S1's MISO/CS (1/0) are header pins.
- One sensor per I2C bus means the default address **0x52** is kept on both; no
  LPn-based address reassignment is needed.
- I2C runs at **400 kHz** (Rev 4). With the trimmed ~320 B frame a frame read is
  (4 × 9) + (320 × 9) = 2916 bits ≈ **7.3–7.4 ms**, so two I2C sensors plus three
  SPI reads take ~17.6 ms of a 66.7 ms frame (≈26 % per I2C bus, counting the
  per-pass `check_data_ready` polls). 1 MHz is no longer needed. The old "1 MHz is
  required" claim was correct for the **untrimmed** 1444 B frame — 2 × 32.6 ms =
  65.2 ms of 66.7 ms — and is obsolete after the output trim.

### 13.4 Hardware deltas for Mode B (not yet drawn)

1. **Bidirectional I2C translation.** The SN74AXC4T774 is unidirectional per
   channel; SDA needs a bidirectional translator — the **TCA9406** (Fm+, VCCA
   1.65–3.6 V, VCCB 2.3–5.5 V). A PCA9306 is **not** acceptable: TI specifies it
   to 400 kHz only, and its internal pull-ups are not enough for a harness.
2. **Sensor mode select.** `SPI_I2C_N` is pulled up (SPI). For I2C it must be
   low — add a jumper or a per-variant resistor.
3. **Header** grows to expose SDA/SCL if one board supports both modes.
4. **SPI2 is on the Teensy 4.0 bottom pads (34–37)**, not the headers.
5. **Wire1 alt pins 36/37 collide with SPI2** — use Wire1 on 16/17 only.
6. **Pin 13 is the LED**; SCK on 13 makes it flicker (harmless).
7. **SYNC (B1)** is still unrouted. Route it if hardware-aligned start-of-frame
   is required; otherwise concurrency is software-only.
8. **I2C pull-ups:** external, sensor side to 1.8 V and host side to 3.3 V; the
   TCA9406 has 10 kΩ internal pull-ups on all four pins that cannot be disabled,
   so fit 2.2 kΩ both rails (effective 1.8 kΩ) and drop to 1.5 kΩ above ~150 pF.
9. **I2C_RST must be allowed low** for I2C — the library drives it low at
   `begin()`. The current board pulls it high (SPI). Tie low or route to a GPIO.
10. **SPI_I2C_N** low = I2C. A fixed pull-down removes the bus-reset capability
    UM3109 describes; a GPIO is better.
11. **Wire buffer:** Teensy `BUFFER_LENGTH` is 136 (tx 137), so the library's
    default chunk (136, plus 2 address bytes) overflows. The cap
    (`DEFAULT_I2C_BUFFER_LEN 128`) is set in
    `vl53l8cx_platform_config_custom.h` under `#if defined(__IMXRT1062__)`, not
    in the sketch, because `IO_Read`/`IO_Write` are inline members compiled in
    every translation unit that includes `vl53l8cx.h`.
12. **I2C translator: TCA9406.** Rated to Fm+ (1 MHz) and used here at 400 kHz, which is ample margin. A PCA9306 is not acceptable (400 kHz part). Use the **TCA9406** (TI: 2-bit bidirectional
    1 MHz I2C/SMBus translator, VCCA 1.65-3.6 V, VCCB 2.3-5.5 V, VCCA <= VCCB,
    internal 10 kOhm pull-ups, high-Z when OE low, VCC isolation). Still scope
    the bus and size external pull-ups from the I2C formulas.
13. **SYNC is a per-acquisition trigger** (UM3109 §4.15), not a start-of-frame
    align. Do not tie all five together expecting simultaneity; use
    separate/staggered lines for time-division.

#### Mode C (five SPI masters via FlexIO) — refuted, not built

The reviewer confirmed the analysis and rejected the performance argument:

- Sampling is already concurrent in every mode; FlexIO only adds physical bus
  isolation. The transfer is CPU-polled byte-by-byte on every bus, so the total
  CPU time does not fall.
- `FlexIOSPI` cannot do mode 3 without patching: `_dataMode` is stored and never
  programmed, and `begin()` hard-codes SCK-idle-low / TX-on-falling /
  RX-on-rising. An external inverter yields mode 2, not mode 3.
- With the trimmed 320-byte frame, Mode A needs ~5 ms of a 66 ms period.

Decision: **Mode A is the production topology; Mode B is the concurrent
variant.** Mode C is dropped. If per-sensor bus isolation is ever required
without I2C, use bit-banged SPI mode 3 on GPIO rather than `FlexIOSPI`.

#### Mode B verdict (round 7)

A trimmed frame read is 0.97 ms on SPI at 3 MHz, so a worst-case Mode A pass with
all five ready is **4.35 ms = 6.5 %** of a 66.7 ms frame; 8×8 tops out at 15 Hz.
Mode A's sequential polling was never the bottleneck, and the trim removed Mode
B's only real advantage. **Do not fabricate the I2C carrier variant.** Mode B's
costs: TCA9406 + pull-up engineering per I2C carrier, two strap changes, bottom-pad
soldering for SPI1/SPI2 regardless, plus an extra failure class (stuck SDA). The
`mode_b` sketch and §13.4 deltas stay as documented options. If isolation is ever
needed, the next step is **`SPI` + `SPI1`** (3+2, identical carriers), not I2C.

#### Also settled this round

- `is_alive()` on a streaming sensor is **harmless**: it only switches the host-interface
  page (0x7FFF) and reads ID registers; the ranging MCU, frame buffers and the
  driver's `streamcount`/`data_read_size` are untouched.
- `init()`'s block configuration cannot go stale: it reboots and re-downloads,
  `start_ranging()` rewrites the output list/enables, and it **verifies** the
  resulting `data_read_size` against the host value (api.c 612–617).
- `MAX_RESULTS_SIZE` 328 < `TEMPORARY_BUFFER_SIZE` 1024, so the trim does not
  under-size `temp_buffer`; every `temp_buffer` consumer is bounded independently.
- Teensy `requestFrom` returns `uint8_t` in all five overloads, so the existing
  cast is a no-op; `BUFFER_LENGTH` 136 / tx 137, and 2 + 128 = 130 fits.

**TPS22918-Q1 facts now verified** (`reference docs/components/tps22918-q1.md`):

- Pins DBV: **1 VIN, 2 GND, 3 ON, 4 CT, 5 QOD, 6 VOUT**.
- **ON V_IH ≥ 1 V, V_IL ≤ 0.5 V**, and ON "cannot be left floating" ⇒ the
  100 kΩ pull-down (R10) is mandatory, not optional.
- **R_ON** at 3.3 V: 53 mΩ typ, **59 mΩ max at 25 °C**, **107 mΩ max over
  −40…+105 °C**. Drop at 750 mA: ≤ 44 mV typ, **≤ 80 mV worst case**.
  The earlier "≤ 78 mΩ ⇒ 59 mV" figure was optimistic; use 107 mΩ.
- **t_R (CT open) = 95 µs at 3.3 V.** Note the switching tables' t_ON 1430 µs /
  t_R 1680 µs / t_D 590 µs are the **CT = 1000 pF** condition, not ours; the
  CT = 0 column of the rise-time table is the relevant one, and t_D at CT = 0
  is not tabulated. The firmware's 5 ms rail-settle wait covers any plausible
  value. The table is valid when VIN is already steady before ON is asserted,
  which is exactly this design (bulk upstream, ON from a GPIO).
- **QOD is a pin, not a feature that is always on.** Options: tie QOD to VOUT
  (internal R_PD), add R_EXT, or leave open (disabled). **This design must tie
  pin 5 to VOUT** — otherwise the "rail cycle recovers a hung sensor" claim is
  false. R_PD = 25 Ω at 3.3 V ⇒ 36.5 µF decays with τ ≈ 0.9 ms.
- Current: 2 A continuous at T_A = 70 °C, **1.5 A at 85 °C** — still above the
  750 mA array peak. C_IN ≥ 1 µF per the recommended operating conditions.
- CT if ever fitted: ≥ 25 V rated (the text says 25 V; the table says 16 V for
  1.2–4 V — use 25 V).

**Consequence:** R1 is closed on paper; the bench still scopes t_R under the real
36.5 µF load (datasheet t_R assumes C_L = 0.1 µF). QOD-to-VOUT is a new layout
requirement and is now in the guide's system-power step and do-not list.

#### Final load-switch configuration (decided, Rev 7)

| Pin | Net | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| 1 VIN | `3V3_IN` | Supply side: bulk ≥ 47 µF and C10 22 µF to GND |
| 2 GND | `GND` | Star point at the supply |
| 3 ON | `RAIL_EN` | Teensy pin 2; **R10 100 kΩ to GND** at the switch |
| 4 CT | — | **No connection, no footprint.** Left open permanently |
| 5 QOD | `3V3_EXT` | **Strapped directly to pin 6** (no R_EXT) |
| 6 VOUT | `3V3_EXT` | C11 1 µF to GND; then to J1-1 of all five carriers |

Reasoning, so it is not reopened later:

- **CT is not a tuning knob here.** 275 µs is an *upper* bound on the rail edge,
  and CT only makes the edge slower. If the measured CORE_1V8 window (≤ 58.3 µs)
  fails, no CT value fixes it — that is an LDO turn-on problem, and the fix is a
  different LDO or accepting the tracking case. Fit nothing on pin 4.
- **QOD direct-tie, no R_EXT.** 25 Ω typ at 3.3 V discharges 36.5 µF with
  τ ≈ 0.9 ms and a 132 mA peak, well inside the pin's design intent. Leaving the
  pin open disables discharge entirely, and a hung sensor may draw too little to
  collapse the rail on its own — the rail cycle would silently stop working.
- **100 kΩ on ON is mandatory.** ON has no internal pull-down and the datasheet
  says it must not float; 0.1 µA leakage × 100 kΩ = 10 mV, far below V_IL 0.5 V.
  Place R10 at the switch, not at the Teensy.
